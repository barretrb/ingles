Possessive forms express the idea of 'having' (in a very general sense) which exists between two nouns. We normally use a possessive noun (+ 's) when something belongs to or is a characteristic of a particular person (people) or thing (things). If there are two people, we put the 's on the second name. If we are referring to things belonging to two separate people, we put the 's on both names, e.g. John's and Kay's tastes in food are completely different. When 's refers to premises, e.g. 'the house of' or 'the shop of', we often omit, e.g. house or shop.
